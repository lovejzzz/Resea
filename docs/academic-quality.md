# Academic quality model

Resea’s quality standard is structural, evidentiary, and review-centered. It does not score prose fluency as a proxy for course quality.

## Evidence quality

A published factual claim must resolve to an evidence unit in the active project. Each evidence unit retains exact text, a source ID, a locator, review state, and retrieval metadata. Sources retain access, license, volatility, and next-review information. Public access never automatically becomes an open-license status.

Source triage uses an explainable signal only to organize review. Authority varies by claim type: official guidance can establish a policy’s content, primary research can support an empirical result, synthesis can characterize a body of literature, curricular sources can show common teaching patterns, and pedagogical guidance can inform design. No type is a universal hierarchy.

## Backward design

Required outcomes use observable actions and identify the capability, conditions, and success criteria. Every outcome must map to:

- prerequisite-ready concepts;
- feedback-bearing practice;
- an assessment that elicits the capability;
- expected evidence or rubric dimensions.

The deterministic audit targets 100% outcome–assessment coverage, 100% outcome–practice coverage, and zero orphan assessments. These metrics reveal missing structure; they do not prove that an assessment is valid.

## Publication

Critical findings block publication. Current critical rules cover missing references, unassessed outcomes, orphan assessments, prerequisite cycles, manifest integrity, and missing expert review for high-stakes configurations.

High findings should be resolved before learner use. Medium and advisory findings remain visible in exports and version records. Passing the gate means “structurally eligible for instructor publication,” not “certified correct.”

## Fixture policy

The packaged social-science methods course is evaluation and onboarding data, not production subject logic. It demonstrates:

- qualified jurisdictional framing around research ethics;
- an exact evidence-to-claim trail;
- measurable outcomes;
- aligned practice and assessment;
- explicit tool-use and accessibility pathways;
- realistic non-critical findings requiring human review.

Production planning derives question families from the instructor’s brief and contains no subject-specific topic branches.
